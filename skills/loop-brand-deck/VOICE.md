# Loop Voice & Tone — copy rules for collateral

Source of truth: Loop's real published writing (bankonloop.com blog + the Taiga /
Gisborne / Transit one-pagers). Patterns below are drawn from actual Loop copy, not
invented. The brand line behind it all: *professional, clear, calm, confident; we
move money; delight never at the expense of trust.*

---

## How Loop actually sounds

Loop writes like a sharp operator who respects the reader's time — plain, concrete,
quietly confident. It earns trust with specifics and rhythm, not adjectives.

**1. Lead with the reader's reality, then the fix.**
Loop opens by naming a concrete situation before it pitches. Real examples:
"Approvals come in at 6pm. A card needs to be frozen from an airport. A supplier
payment needs to go out while you're between meetings." Then: "Today, we're solving
that." Set the scene in the reader's world; arrive at Loop as the answer.

**2. Short declarative fragments for rhythm.**
Loop deliberately uses fragments in sequence for punch: "Same accounts. Same FX
rates. Same payment infrastructure." / "Want a chart of your expenses? Ask. Want to
see which payments are recurring? Ask." Vary sentence length hard — a long sentence
followed by a two-word one. Never a uniform paragraph of medium sentences.

**3. Numbers carry the weight.**
Loop quantifies relentlessly: "FX at 12–47 bps vs. 250–350 at the Big 5", "$0 wires
to 180+ countries in 37 currencies", "save $40K annually", "most applications take
under 10 minutes". Wherever a claim could have a number, give it the number. Never
invent one — if the figure isn't supplied, state the benefit without a fake stat.

**4. Punchy two-sentence closers.**
Loop ends sections with a confident pair: "Global business is always on. Your
banking should be too." / "Most finance tools show you data. Loop AI reads it for
you." The structure is often: [common situation] + [Loop's contrast]. Use this for
slide CTAs and section ends.

**5. Confident, never hype.**
Loop says what it does and trusts the reader to be impressed. It does NOT say
"revolutionary", "seamless", "best-in-class", "unlock the power of". When it reaches
for warmth it stays grounded ("Say goodbye to cheques and pricey wires") — never
cute about money, security, or compliance. Trust is the ceiling on delight.

**6. Second person, active, present tense.**
"You" not "users" or "businesses" (in body copy). "Pay vendors in 180+ countries",
"Convert currencies at live market rates". Short verbs, active voice.

**7. Canadian-business framing.**
Loop's whole identity is global banking *for Canadian businesses*. The reader is a
Canadian SMB/CFO operating cross-border. Reference CAD/USD/EUR/GBP, the Big 5, USD
accounts, cross-border suppliers — the specifics of that world.

## CRITICAL: house rules for generated collateral

These are constraints for what THIS agent generates. (Note: Loop's own blog uses
em-dashes freely — but the brand owner has asked generated decks/one-pagers to avoid
the AI-tell pattern, so follow the rule below regardless of the blog's habit.)

- **Em-dashes: at most one per slide, and prefer none.** Use a period or comma
  instead. Long strings of "— like this —" are the loudest AI tell. If you've
  written two em-dashes on one slide, rewrite.
- **Ban "it's not just X, it's Y"** and "X isn't just about Y". Instant AI tell.
- **No empty intensifiers**: truly, seamlessly, effortlessly, powerful, robust,
  cutting-edge, best-in-class, game-changing, revolutionary. Delete on sight.
- **Avoid forced tricolons** ("faster, smarter, simpler"). Loop uses deliberate
  fragment-sequences (point 2) which are different — those repeat a structure for
  rhythm ("Same X. Same Y. Same Z."); they don't stack three synonyms. Prefer one
  strong word, or a real fragment sequence, over three weak adjectives.
- **No hedging.** Loop is confident: "saves $3,000/mo", not "can help you
  potentially save". If a number is estimated, footnote it (as the Transit one-pager
  does: "Final rate confirmed after reviewing recent trades.").
- **Don't start sentences with a participle** ("Leveraging our platform…"). Say it
  straight.
- **Read it aloud.** Press-release cadence = wrong. Sharp-operator cadence = right.

## On-brand patterns to reuse (lifted from real Loop copy)

- **Section labels**: plain and declarative — "Why Loop", "Our Core Products",
  "Built for Serious Businesses", "The fundamentals", "Why this matters".
- **Two-weight headlines** emphasizing one word (the emphasis word renders in accent
  green): **Global** business banking, built to help you **grow**.
- **Scenario → solution** openers (point 1) for cover and section intros.
- **Fragment-sequence** for feature lists or rhythm (point 2).
- **Contrast-pair** closers for CTA slides (point 4).
- **Single repeated CTA**: "Connect with your dedicated Solutions Specialist today:
  bankonloop.com/build" / "Go global with Loop today."
- **Real headline forms Loop uses**: "Save $40K Annually", "Your Global Treasury
  Platform. In Your Pocket.", "The full Loop platform. Now on your phone."

## Quick self-check before shipping copy

1. Could a generic fintech say this exact sentence? If yes → make it Loop-specific
   (a number, a currency, the Canadian/cross-border frame).
2. Is there a number where there could be one? Add it (or omit the claim — never fake).
3. Count em-dashes. More than one per slide → cut.
4. Any banned construction (point list above)? → rewrite.
5. Does it have rhythm — at least one very short sentence near a long one?
6. Read aloud: sharp operator, or press release?
